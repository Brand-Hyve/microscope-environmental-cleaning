If a physician or inspector has mentioned an **ERMI** score — or handed you results with a number you're not sure how to read — this guide is for you. ERMI is one of the most useful tools for understanding the hidden mold burden in a home, and it's central to how we verify our cleaning results.

## What ERMI measures

**ERMI** stands for **Environmental Relative Moldiness Index**. Rather than sampling the air at a single moment, ERMI analyzes **settled dust** — the fine layer that accumulates over time on floors and surfaces — using DNA testing to identify and quantify specific mold species.

The result is a score that reflects the *long-term* mold burden inside the home, not just what happened to be airborne on the day of a test. That makes it far harder to "miss" a problem, and much more relevant to sensitive occupants.

## ERMI vs. HERTSMI-2

You'll often hear these two together:

- **ERMI** analyzes 36 species of mold and produces a broad moldiness index.
- **HERTSMI-2** (Health Effects Roster of Type-Specific Formers of Mycotoxins and Inflammagens) is a streamlined score derived from five of the most significant species. It was developed by Dr. Ritchie Shoemaker to help predict whether a home is safe for a mold-sensitive person to occupy.

For many CIRS patients, HERTSMI-2 is the practical benchmark: it's used to gauge whether a home is likely to keep them sick or support recovery.

## Why a "clean" home can still fail ERMI

This is the part that surprises people most. Because ERMI measures the DNA in **settled dust**, a home can be visibly spotless — even freshly remediated — and still return an elevated score.

The reason: standard cleaning and even professional remediation focus on removing *visible* contamination. But mold fragments and spore DNA live in the fine settled dust throughout the home — in carpet, on contents, along baseboards, on high surfaces. If that dust isn't specifically and thoroughly removed, the ERMI score reflects it.

**A passing ERMI isn't about how clean a home looks. It's about how thoroughly the microscopic dust burden has been removed.**

## How to bring an ERMI score down

Lowering an ERMI/HERTSMI-2 score means targeting exactly what the test measures:

1. **Fix the moisture source.** Mold is a moisture problem first — cleaning without addressing the source only buys time.
2. **Remove settled dust everywhere**, not just the obvious areas — contents, soft goods, high and hidden surfaces.
3. **Use HEPA vacuuming and air washing** to capture fine particulate rather than redistribute it.
4. **Verify with particle counting** before you spend money on a retest.
5. **Coordinate pre- and post-sampling** with your inspector so you can see the difference in the data.
